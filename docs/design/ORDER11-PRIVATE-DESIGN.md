# Order 11: design brief for the private application

**Status:** proposal for the owner's review (2026-10-20). Section 10 of the visual identity describes the customer application and
operations as "a calmer, functional register" with "no decorative objects". This brief keeps that register and adds the few elements below.
They change how a surface is structured, so by §12.6 of the identity they need the owner's approval: the owner's review of the screens is
that approval, element by element.

## 1. The path

Kauro is one path, walked with the customer from the first visit to the first project and on to the next one:

1. **Public site:** the promise, drawn as a window of the product.
2. **Contact:** the fit form.
3. **Account:** sign-in and setup.
4. **The project's six stages:** upload and seal, the free data health check, review and approve the plan, quote and payment, preparation
   and verification, delivery and the team's import.
5. **The next project:** the repeat discount, the same team, the same habits.

Every private page knows where it sits on this path and says what comes next. The words are the ones the public site already taught: the
six stages are named exactly as on How it works, in the same order, with the same split of who acts (your team or Kauro).

## 2. The application fulfils the picture

The public Home shows the product as a window:

- a carbon bar with the wordmark, the project's name and its state in a pill;
- a progress list with wine check rings and its facts right-aligned;
- the records in mono.

Inside the application that picture becomes the working surface. A project is drawn as that window, and the stage list is the progress
list. What a visitor saw on the public site is what a customer then uses. Nothing is a figure: every value is the project's own.

## 3. Personality, in doses

| Element | Where | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eyebrow with a rule | Above an h1: the workspace or the section | Small caps, muted, the same as on the public site |
| Two-weight heading | Only on the pages that open a path: setup, the projects board, the private home | The registry's own sentence; light words in the primary colour |
| Check rings | Stage lists, setup steps, readiness lists | Wine when done, outlined when waiting; always with a word |
| Stamp | Only on a verdict: an invitation accepted or expired, a set sealed, a plan approved, a package delivered | One per page at most; it never replaces the words |
| Editor's note | Where a person may hesitate: what a choice changes, who acts next | Serif italic, at most one per page |
| Carbon window bar | The project's own frame, and the objects that stand for a real artifact (an invitation, a quote) | Never as a decoration |

Removed on purpose:

- panels with an empty half;
- placeholder art;
- counts nobody asked for;
- a second primary action on a page.

## 4. Three applications, one hand

| Who | What the application is for | Home | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agency or company** | Getting one client's data, then the next, prepared and imported safely, with the team's roles respected | The projects board: what needs us now, what Kauro is doing, what is ready to import, what is done | Calm guidance; one next step at a time |
| **Kauro owner** | Running the company: decisions only the owner takes, money in and out, the health of the work, releases | Today: the queues that need the owner, the facts behind each | Exact values, refusals named, no customer CRM data |
| **Seller** | Bringing agencies honestly, under reviewed messages, and seeing what was earned and why | My work: prospects, agencies, statements | Plain, rule-bound, never pushy |

A direct company sees the same application as an agency, with one workspace instead of a list of clients. The words speak of the company
(this needs a copy variant: dependency D-P02-1).

## 5. Rules every private page keeps

1. One h1. An eyebrow says where the page is; a lead says what the page is for, in the registry's own sentence.
2. One primary action, the next step. Everything else is secondary or a link.
3. Facts as lists with hairlines; numbers in tabular figures; references and hashes in mono, wrapping.
4. Every state is a panel with a heading, its words and one next step. No state is shown by colour alone.
5. Nothing the contract does not hold is presented as real. A proposed field is marked once, quietly, where it appears.
6. 320 to 1440 px, both themes, keyboard first, reduced motion. No horizontal scroll; tables become cards on a phone.
